How to Compare Two Cars Properly Before You Buy

A clear car comparison checklist covering safety, space, performance, comfort, running costs, warranty and after-sales support.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

Compare the exact variants

A model name alone is not enough. Features, safety equipment, powertrain and price can change significantly between variants and model years. Compare the exact variants you can actually buy in your location.

Prioritise safety

Review the safety equipment offered on each variant, including airbags, electronic stability control, braking aids and child-seat anchor provisions where applicable. Look for credible, model- and variant-specific crash-test information and check the test protocol and year. Do not assume a rating for one version automatically applies to every version.

Check everyday usability

Consider rear-seat comfort, boot space, visibility, ingress and egress, parking dimensions and ease of fitting child seats or luggage. If possible, take the people who will regularly travel in the car for the test drive.

Compare ownership costs

Estimate fuel or electricity use, insurance, scheduled service, tyres and warranty coverage. Ask about service intervals, parts availability and the authorised service network near your home or workplace.

Make a weighted shortlist

Write down your five most important needs and rank them. A family that drives mostly in a city may value visibility, comfort and parking ease; a frequent highway traveller may prioritise stability, seats and driver assistance. Use a side-by-side comparison, then test-drive the finalists.
