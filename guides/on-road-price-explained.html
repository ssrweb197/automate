Ex-Showroom vs On-Road Price: What Car Buyers Should Know

Understand the difference between ex-showroom and on-road car prices, the common cost components and how to compare dealer quotations.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

What is the ex-showroom price?

The ex-showroom price is the listed vehicle price before the buyer completes local registration and other delivery-related requirements. It is useful for comparing a model's advertised starting price, but it is not usually the final amount payable to take the car home.

What can be included in the on-road price?

An on-road quotation commonly combines the ex-showroom amount with registration and road tax, insurance, and other applicable charges. Optional accessories, extended warranty products, service packages or handling-related line items may also appear. Their availability and treatment vary by state and dealer.

Ask for an itemised quotation

Request every line item separately. Ask which items are mandatory, which are optional, and which can be purchased independently. Compare quotations for the same model, variant, registration location, insurance coverage and add-ons. A lower headline amount may simply exclude items included in another quote.

Why does the price vary by location?

Registration rules, road tax and insurance choices can differ by state and city. Vehicle variant, fuel type, engine size, electric-vehicle incentives and policy changes may also affect the final amount. Always use a current quotation for your registration location.

Before you pay

Check the vehicle variant and model year, insurance coverage, registration details, delivery commitments and refund terms. Do not treat an online estimate as a final invoice. Use AutoMate's on-road budget planner for an initial estimate, then verify it with the dealer and relevant authorities.
