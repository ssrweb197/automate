Car Safety Features Explained: What Buyers Should Check

Understand common active and passive car safety features and how to verify which equipment is included in a specific variant.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

Passive safety

Seat belts, airbags, head restraints and a well-engineered passenger cell help reduce injury risk in a crash. Check the number and placement of airbags on the exact variant rather than relying on a model's top-spec brochure.

Active safety

Features such as anti-lock braking systems and electronic stability control can help drivers maintain control in challenging conditions. Some vehicles also offer traction control, emergency braking assistance or advanced driver-assistance systems. Availability varies by model and trim.

Crash-test information

When reviewing a crash-test result, confirm the exact model, body style, production specification, test year and assessment protocol. Ratings from different programmes may not be directly comparable. A rating should be considered alongside equipment and responsible driving.

Child and pedestrian considerations

Check for child-seat anchor compatibility, rear-door child locks and clear instructions for installing restraints. Visibility, lighting and driver-assistance functions may also matter to other road users.

Verify before purchase

Ask the dealer to demonstrate safety features and show the variant equipment list. Read the owner's manual and manufacturer documentation. Do not assume a feature is standard across every trim or model year.
