Petrol, Diesel, Hybrid or Electric: How to Choose a Powertrain

A practical guide to choosing a petrol, diesel, hybrid or electric car based on driving patterns, charging access, travel needs and total ownership.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

Start with your driving pattern

Estimate how far you drive on a typical day, how often you make long trips, and how much time is spent in city traffic. Your actual usage matters more than a broad claim that one powertrain is always cheaper.

Petrol and diesel

Petrol vehicles may suit buyers who value broad availability and a familiar refuelling routine. Diesel can be worth evaluating for some high-mileage or highway-heavy users, but local regulations, purchase price, maintenance and resale expectations should be considered. Check current rules in your city.

Hybrid options

Hybrid systems combine an engine with electric assistance in different ways. Their efficiency and driving behaviour vary by technology and vehicle. Understand whether the car is a mild hybrid, strong hybrid or plug-in hybrid and what that means for your usage.

Electric vehicles

An EV can be convenient when home or workplace charging is practical and daily distances fit the vehicle's real-world range. Consider charging access on regular routes, public charger reliability, charging time, battery warranty and long-distance travel plans.

Compare total cost and convenience

Look beyond fuel or electricity price. Include purchase price, financing, insurance, maintenance, tyres, charging equipment and expected resale. Use realistic local energy prices and verify current vehicle specifications before deciding.
