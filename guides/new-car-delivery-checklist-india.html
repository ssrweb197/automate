New Car Delivery Checklist in India: 20 Things to Check Before Taking Delivery

A practical pre-delivery inspection (PDI) guide to help you check the vehicle, confirm the details you ordered and review important handover documents.

Updated 17 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

Why a pre-delivery inspection matters

Taking delivery of a new car is an exciting milestone. Before accepting the vehicle, set aside enough time to inspect it in good lighting and review the paperwork. A pre-delivery inspection can help you notice visible transport damage, missing items, differences from your order or questions that need the dealer's attention.

Where possible, arrange the inspection before final registration or handover and ask the dealership to explain its process. The checks below are general buyer guidance; the exact procedure and documents can vary by vehicle, manufacturer, dealer and state.

Part 1: Inspect the exterior

  1. Walk around the entire vehicle. Look at every body panel in daylight for scratches, dents, paint marks, uneven panel gaps or signs of repair.
  2. Check bumpers and lower edges. Inspect the front and rear bumpers, door edges, sills and accessible underside areas for scuffs or damage.
  3. Inspect glass and mirrors. Look for chips or cracks in the windshield, windows, rear glass and mirrors. Check that mirror adjustment works if electrically operated.
  4. Examine tyres and wheels. Check for cuts, bulges or visible damage. Confirm the tyre size and specification against the vehicle information supplied by the manufacturer.
  5. Test exterior lights. With help from another person, check headlamps, tail lamps, indicators, brake lights, hazard lights and fog lamps if fitted.

Part 2: Check the cabin and equipment

  1. Inspect seats and trim. Check seat fabric or leather, stitching, dashboard, door pads, roof lining and carpets for stains, tears or marks.
  2. Test seat adjustments and belts. Operate the available seat adjustments and check that seat belts latch and retract normally.
  3. Check air conditioning. Run the fan at different speeds and test the available temperature and airflow settings.
  4. Try infotainment and connectivity. Check the display, audio, steering controls, Bluetooth or phone connectivity and cameras or sensors where fitted.
  5. Operate convenience features. Test power windows, central locking, wipers, washer jets, horn, boot release and adjustable mirrors where available.

Part 3: Confirm vehicle identity and basic operation

  1. Match the VIN. Check the vehicle identification number on the car against the invoice and relevant documents. Ask the dealer to show you the identification locations for your model.
  2. Confirm model and variant. Verify the make, model, variant, colour, transmission and fuel or powertrain match your booking and final invoice.
  3. Check the odometer. Note the displayed reading and ask the dealer to explain any reading that seems unusual. Some movement during storage, inspection or logistics may occur.
  4. Start the vehicle safely. Follow the dealer's instructions to start the engine or activate an EV's ready mode. Ask about any warning message that remains displayed after the normal start-up checks.
  5. Review supplied emergency items. Confirm the items applicable to your model, such as a spare wheel or tyre repair kit, jack, tool kit, warning equipment and EV charging cable or accessories.

Part 4: Review documents, insurance and payments

  1. Read the final invoice. Check the variant, colour, vehicle details, agreed price, discounts, accessories and any additional charges against the written quotation.
  2. Verify insurance details. Confirm the insured vehicle details, policy period, coverage, declared value and add-ons you selected. Ask for the policy document and clarify anything you do not understand.
  3. Check registration paperwork. Review the registration-related documents and confirm what has been submitted, what is pending and how you will receive the final records. Requirements and processes can differ by state.
  4. Collect warranty and service information. Ask when the manufacturer warranty starts, what it covers, what exclusions apply and how to access the service schedule and authorised service network.
  5. Collect all handover items. Obtain all supplied keys, owner's manual, service information, payment receipts, warranty details and written confirmation of any promised accessory or pending work.

Questions to ask the dealer before accepting delivery

  • Has the vehicle completed the dealership's pre-delivery inspection?
  • Does the VIN on the vehicle match the invoice and documents?
  • Are all accessories and items listed in the order included?
  • When does the warranty begin, and where can I read its terms?
  • Are any repairs, software updates, documents or accessory installations still pending?
  • Who should I contact if I notice a concern after leaving the dealership?

What to do if you find a problem

Do not ignore a scratch, damaged component, missing item or document mismatch. Take clear photographs, describe the issue and ask the dealership to record it in writing. Request a specific resolution and timeline. For a significant concern, consider postponing acceptance until you have received an explanation and are satisfied with the proposed solution. Keep copies of messages, inspection notes and receipts.

Final thoughts

Take your time during the handover. Inspect the vehicle in good light, compare it with your order, read the documents and ask the dealer to resolve unanswered questions. A calm, documented checklist can make the delivery process clearer and help you begin ownership with confidence.
