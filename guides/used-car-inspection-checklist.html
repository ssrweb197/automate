Used Car Inspection Checklist: What to Check Before Paying

A careful checklist for evaluating a used car, including documents, service history, body condition, tyres, test drive and independent inspection.

Updated 14 November 2026 · Independent buyer education

Verify the documents

Check the registration certificate details, seller identity, insurance, pollution certificate where applicable, pending loan or hypothecation status and any required transfer documents. Use official transport services or the relevant authority to verify records. Never rely only on screenshots supplied by a seller.

Inspect the vehicle in daylight

Look for uneven paint, panel gaps, corrosion, water damage, cracked glass, tyre wear and signs of repairs. Check the cabin for unusual odours, damp carpets, warning lights and worn controls. A clean appearance does not prove mechanical condition.

Review service and ownership history

Ask for service invoices, maintenance records, accident disclosures, warranty details and the number of previous owners. Confirm that the vehicle identification details match the documents and vehicle.

Take a proper test drive

Drive only with permission and valid documentation. Listen for unusual sounds and observe braking, steering, transmission, suspension and warning lights. Do not attempt risky tests.

Arrange an independent inspection

A qualified mechanic or trusted inspection service can identify issues that a casual buyer may miss. Obtain a written estimate for repairs and factor it into the price. Complete payment and ownership transfer using safe, documented steps.
