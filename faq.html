Car buying FAQs

Clear, practical answers to common questions about researching, comparing and owning a car. Use these answers as a starting point and confirm model-specific details, prices and legal requirements with the relevant manufacturer, dealer, insurer or authority.

Choosing and buying a car

1. How should I decide my budget before buying a car?

Start with the total on-road cost you can comfortably afford, not only the advertised ex-showroom price. Include the down payment, loan repayment, insurance, fuel or charging, maintenance, parking and other regular expenses. Keep an emergency buffer rather than using all available savings.

2. What is the difference between ex-showroom price and on-road price?

Ex-showroom price is the vehicle price before several location- and buyer-specific charges. On-road price is the amount quoted to get the car registered and ready for use, commonly including applicable taxes or registration charges, insurance and selected add-ons. Ask the dealer for an itemised written quotation.

Read our on-road price guide.

3. How many cars should I shortlist before visiting dealerships?

Shortlist around three to five cars that fit your budget and actual needs. Compare the exact variants, safety equipment, comfort, running costs, service support and warranty. Then test-drive the strongest candidates.

4. Which car body type is best for a family?

There is no single best body type for every family. Check the number of regular passengers, child-seat fitment, rear-seat comfort, boot space, ease of entry and parking conditions. Test the exact model with the people and luggage you normally carry.

5. What should I check during a car test drive?

Check driving position, visibility, seat comfort, steering, braking feel, acceleration, gear shifts, suspension over familiar road surfaces, cabin noise, air-conditioning and parking visibility. Drive in conditions similar to your daily use, where possible.

Use our test-drive checklist.

6. Should I buy the base variant or a higher variant?

Compare the actual feature and safety-equipment list for each model year. Pay particular attention to airbags, electronic stability control, braking aids, parking aids and features you will use regularly. Choose a higher variant only when its additional equipment justifies the price for your needs.

Comparing specifications and powertrains

7. How do I compare two car variants correctly?

Compare like-for-like variants and model years. Check engine or motor, transmission, safety equipment, dimensions, seating, boot space, warranty, service schedule and the final on-road quotation. A model name alone may cover significantly different equipment levels.

See our car comparison checklist.

8. Is petrol, diesel, hybrid or electric better?

The right option depends on annual distance, typical trip length, fuel or electricity access, charging availability, purchase-price difference and local running costs. Estimate your own usage over several years instead of relying only on claimed mileage or range.

Read our powertrain comparison guide.

9. Is a higher claimed mileage always better?

Not necessarily. Real-world efficiency varies with traffic, speed, driving style, load, terrain, air-conditioning and vehicle condition. Use official test-cycle figures for reference, but ask owners or conduct a suitable test drive to understand likely use in your conditions.

10. Where can I verify a car's safety rating?

Look for the latest rating from a recognised crash-test programme and confirm that it applies to the same model generation, market and relevant variant. Safety equipment can differ between versions. Also check the vehicle's standard safety features and official documentation.

11. Are dimensions and boot-space figures enough to judge practicality?

No. Published measurements are useful for initial comparison, but seat shape, door opening, floor height, cabin layout and boot shape affect everyday use. Inspect the actual vehicle and try fitting your regular luggage or child seats.

Loans, insurance and ownership costs

12. How is car loan EMI calculated?

EMI generally depends on the amount borrowed, interest rate and repayment tenure. A longer tenure can reduce the monthly instalment but may increase total interest. Compare the lender's annual rate, processing fees, foreclosure terms and total repayment before accepting an offer.

Try the AutoMate EMI calculator for an estimate and read our car loan guide.

13. Should I make a larger down payment?

A larger down payment reduces the loan principal and may lower interest paid, but do not drain your emergency savings to achieve it. Compare the cost of borrowing with the need to keep enough cash for essential expenses and unexpected events.

14. What is included in car insurance?

Coverage depends on the policy type, insured vehicle, add-ons, exclusions and policy wording. Review third-party cover, own-damage cover, insured declared value, deductibles, exclusions, cashless garage network and claim conditions before buying or renewing.

Read our car insurance basics guide.

15. What is total cost of ownership?

Total cost of ownership looks beyond the purchase price. It can include depreciation, loan interest, insurance, fuel or electricity, servicing, repairs, tyres, parking and applicable taxes or fees over the period you keep the car.

See our ownership cost guide.

16. How often should I service my car?

Follow the maintenance schedule in the owner's manual and the manufacturer's guidance for your exact model and usage. Severe traffic, dusty roads, extreme temperatures or heavy use may affect maintenance needs. Keep invoices and service records.

17. What documents should I keep after buying a car?

Keep the purchase invoice, registration certificate details, insurance policy, payment records, warranty documents, service invoices and roadside-assistance details where applicable. Store digital copies securely and check that the information is accurate.

Used cars and delivery checks

18. What should I inspect before buying a used car?

Check ownership and registration details, insurance, service history, pending finance or hypothecation status, accident or repair signs, tyres, electrical equipment and test-drive behaviour. Arrange an independent inspection by a qualified mechanic before paying or signing.

Use our used-car inspection checklist.

19. Is a vehicle history report enough to approve a used car?

No. A report may not contain every accident, repair, ownership or service event. Cross-check documents, inspect the vehicle physically, verify records with relevant sources and obtain an independent mechanical inspection.

20. What should I check before accepting a new car delivery?

Inspect the body and glass in good light, verify the VIN and documents, check the odometer, tyres, lights, cabin equipment, keys and accessories, and confirm insurance and registration details. Record any damage or missing item in writing before accepting delivery.

21. Can I rely on the delivery date or manufacturing month alone?

No. Ask the dealer to confirm the manufacturing details and vehicle identification number, then verify them on the vehicle and documents. If the vehicle's age or storage history matters to you, ask for a clear written explanation before proceeding.
